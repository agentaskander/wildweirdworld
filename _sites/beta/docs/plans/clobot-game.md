# Clobot Game Plan

## Concept

Clobot Game is a Roblox-native cozy sci-fi exploration universe about customizable semi-autonomous companion bots. Players build strange little bots for stranger little worlds, then use those bots to explore signal zones, solve environmental puzzles, repair forgotten machine ecosystems, and create playful social spaces.

This is not Clover Stream, not a trading game, not a finance simulator, and not a crypto-native product. It must stand on its own as a game.

The main public Clobot Game page remains the broad game universe: helper bots, weird realms, exploration loops, creator sandbox hooks, co-op missions, and future Roblox expansion. The face creator system lives separately so it does not take over core lore or gameplay.

Comparison routes:

- Classic preserved version: `/clobot-lab-classic`
- Next experiment: `/clobot-lab-next`
- Legacy aliases may continue to point to the next experiment for compatibility.

## Game Pillars

- Customizable helper bots with modular parts, personalities, and behavior chips.
- Weird internet culture translated into safe, playful creature-world design.
- Cozy exploration across machine-nature biomes.
- Emergent bot behavior that feels surprising but understandable.
- Creator tools for bot skins, mini bases, obstacle realms, and signal puzzles.
- Co-op missions where different bot types help each other.
- Cosmetic-only sharing and trading, with moderation and safety rules.

## Target Audience

- Roblox players who like pets, collection, exploration, decorating, and social creativity.
- Kids and tweens who want programmable companions without heavy coding.
- Creators who like weird internet aesthetics but need a parent-safe world.
- Friends who want to build and test playful challenge rooms together.

## Monetization Philosophy

- No trading, investing, speculation, or financial simulation.
- No pay-to-win bot power.
- Favor cosmetic upgrades, skins, base decor, emotes, optional creator packs, and seasonal visual rewards.
- Cosmetic creation should be safe, moderated, and age-appropriate.

## Safety Philosophy

- No public stranger DMs inside the concept.
- No finance systems or market mechanics in gameplay.
- No personal data collection.
- Moderated sharing for player-made bot skins and realms.
- Co-op missions should reward kindness, repair, and creativity.
- Educational coding-lite systems should feel like play, not school pressure.

## Roblox Strategy

- Start with a small hub and one strong bot designer.
- Add three early realms: Glitch Garden, Signal Swamp, and Scraplight Valley.
- Build core bot behaviors first: scout, glow, collect, repair, echo.
- Add co-op doors and signal puzzles that require different bot types.
- Let players decorate mini bases and test their bots in small challenge rooms.

## Creator Economy Strategy

- Player-created cosmetics only.
- Player-created obstacle realms and signal puzzles can be featured through moderation.
- No market dashboard language, no speculation, no stock-chart aesthetics.
- Reward creators with badges, gallery features, seasonal showcases, and safe template packs.

## Experimental Creator Page

Clobot Face Lab is an optional experimental layer, not the core game loop. It lives separately from the main page. It is a sandbox for kids and players to create Clobot face identities, expressive visual styles, personalities, gadgets, stickers, profile cards, bot manuals, and playful mini brands.

The lab should feel like creator tools inside a bigger imaginative universe. It supports Clobot without limiting what Clobot can become.

## Face DNA System

Every Clobot face starts from the same simple emotional anchor:

- A rounded lowercase `c`.
- Two eyes placed inside the open cavity, slightly higher than center.
- Minimal readable geometry.
- A silhouette that works as a Roblox companion, UI icon, sticker, emote, toy, or profile badge.

The lowercase `c` is not a mouth mark and not a logo pasted onto a mascot. It is the actual face container. Expressions evolve from eye spacing, eye shape, glow, antennae, signal rings, accessories, emotion particles, and optional tiny expression overlays.

Rejected wrong design: a C as a mouth, a detached mouth-C, or a random C badge placed on a mascot. The default starting form must clearly match the Clov hero face.

## Approved Face Proportions

- The Next experiment should use a thinner, airier C stroke than the preserved Classic version.
- The C should be circular and open on the right with rounded caps.
- The stroke should feel like a soft neon ring, not a thick mascot blob.
- The interior negative space should remain generous.
- Eyes sit inside the C, centered horizontally as a pair and raised roughly 6-10% above the face center.
- The default face has no mouth. Smiles and marks are optional expression overlays only.

## Forbidden Distortions

- No C as mouth.
- No detached mouth-C.
- No random logo placement on the face, forehead, body, or badge.
- No thick blob C that loses the elegant Clov hero identity.
- No generic emoji face that abandons the lowercase C structure.
- No over-detailed mech face that fails at Roblox distance.

## Geometry Rules

- Prefer stable SVG geometry for the base face.
- Use a path/arc with round line caps instead of a text glyph when rendering the C.
- Keep the viewBox stable for hero, cards, previews, persona tiles, badges, and merch mockups.
- Variant accessories may decorate the face, but should not hide the open right-side gap.
- Preserve the same base geometry across Happy Helper, Sleepy Unit, Glitch Kid, Explorer, Moss Bot, Static Watcher, Echo Tuner, and Scrap Wizard.

## Clobot Face Geometry Rules

- Classic should stay the preserved comparison version, but with a lighter-weight C than the earlier heavy mockup.
- Recommended Classic base is the clean C with eyes and no inner circle.
- The inner circle is optional only when it reads cleanly against the C geometry; otherwise keep it as a comparison tile.
- Next should use a centered, normal lowercase-c arc with a clear but not oversized opening.
- Next may offset the C slightly left for optical centering while keeping the eyes balanced inside the face.
- Top and bottom C endpoints should extend farther around the circle before the opening, so the shape reads as a lowercase c rather than a broken parenthesis.
- Avoid over-closed C shapes; the right-side opening must remain clearly readable.
- Eyes sit slightly above true center, but not too high.
- The base face must always remain lowercase-c plus eyes inside the mark.

## Visual Identity Constraints

- The Clov/Clobot C-face is the starting base and emotional anchor.
- The face system should feel iconic, curious, alive, and kid-friendly without becoming babyish.
- Use minimal geometry, strong silhouettes, soft glow, and clear eye states.
- Preserve experimentation through color, glow, accessories, antennae, auras, stickers, hats, personality chips, and animation.

## Modular Identity System

- Let players choose c-face variants, eye spacing, eye glow, eye shape, optional expression overlays, antennae, tools, movement styles, signal colors, idle animations, and names.
- Use the lowercase `c` structure as the emotional anchor and mascot system.
- Use modular circular design language, signal rings, glowing cores, and friendly robotics as expression tools, not branding overlays.
- Keep room for asymmetry, mossy builds, scrap inventions, strange silhouettes, handmade stickers, and weird internet charm.

## Silhouette Preservation Rules

- The open side of the lowercase `c` should remain readable at small sizes.
- Eyes must sit inside the c cavity, not outside as random face parts.
- Accessories can decorate the shell, but should not hide the c structure.
- Biome variants can add moss, static, gold sparks, glow, or scrap, but the base face should still read quickly.
- Avoid putting a separate C symbol in a mouth, chest, forehead, or random badge position.

## Emotional Readability Principles

- Expressions should read in one glance: happy, sleepy, chaotic, curious, glitchy, focused, mossy, scouting, treasure-excited, or echo-listening.
- Eye changes should do most of the emotional work.
- No mouth is required by default. Any smile or surprise mark is an optional expression overlay.
- Glow intensity, signal particles, and motion can show state changes without adding clutter.
- Clobots should feel like companions that react back, not static avatars.

## Roblox Scaling Principles

- Faces must work while moving, floating, following, or reacting quickly.
- The c silhouette should read in profile icons, overhead badges, small NPC companions, UI buttons, stickers, and plush concepts.
- Avoid over-detailed mech parts that disappear at Roblox camera distance.
- Prefer bold shapes, soft glow, strong contrast, and simple expression states.

## Safe Creator Economy Ideas

- Mock posters, bot cards, creator badges, bot manuals, sticker sheets, fake product ads, and collectible profile cards.
- Player-made mini brands such as repair shops, tiny factory clubs, signal-art studios, and bot families.
- Moderated template remixing and safe design sharing.
- Cosmetic idea publishing only; no real sales system inside the child-facing concept.
- Frame this as imagination and digital creativity, not monetized influencer culture.

## Visual Language Boundaries

- The lowercase c face system helps recognition and can support creator kits or merch later.
- The c face system must not make every Clobot identical.
- The lab should not feel like a rigid avatar project.
- Avoid over-corporatized templates, forced sameness, and brand lock-in.
- Preserve weirdness, experimentation, emergent creativity, asymmetry, charm, and strange inventions.

## Creator Freedom Boundaries

- The face DNA creates a shared starting point, not a prison.
- Clobots can have different bodies, biomes, accessories, gadgets, signal auras, and personalities.
- Some creatures and machines in the broader Clobot universe do not need to be c-faced Clobots.
- The lab should support remixing while protecting the recognizable emotional anchor.
- Avoid generic logo-slap design and mascot-style branding overlays.

## Protecting Creative Diversity

Different creator archetypes should make different things:

- Junkyard Builders.
- Glow Engineers.
- Moss Mechanics.
- Signal Artists.
- Tiny Factory Clubs.
- Scrap Wizards.
- Echo Tuners.

The lab should reward variety. A clean c-face bot, a moss-covered repair crawler, and a lopsided scrap helper should all feel valid.

## Experimental Brand Layer vs Core Lore

The core Clobot universe remains open and expansive: strange worlds, signal creatures, helper bots, environmental puzzles, and emergent exploration.

The face lab is a layer beside that universe. It provides expression templates, identity kits, and creation prompts, but it does not define the laws, lore, or visual identity of every Clobot realm.

## Future Face System Concepts

- Animated faces with blink loops, eye squash, and signal pulse reactions.
- Evolving eye shaders tied to realm discoveries and friendship memory.
- Signal moods such as sleepy after long expeditions, excited near treasure, glitchy in corrupted biomes, mossy in forest realms, and rainbow-static during events.
- AI-lite personality growth where repeated play patterns nudge expressions and idle animations.
- Player reputation reactions, such as warmer greetings after helpful co-op behavior.

## Roblox Engagement Rationale

Collectible faces give players a reason to care about Clobots beyond stats. Expression variants support player identity, naming, remixing, toy-like attachment, profile icons, UI badges, emotes, and future avatar/item creation while keeping the base silhouette readable in motion.

## What Clobot Is NOT

- Not Clover Stream.
- Not a trading game.
- Not a finance simulator.
- Not a stock chart or portfolio UI.
- Not a crypto-native experience.
- Not an investment product.
- Not a Roblox trend clone.
